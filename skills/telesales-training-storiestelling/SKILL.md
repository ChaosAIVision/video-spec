# Telesales Training Storiestelling

Create an expert-led lesson whose core evidence is a realistic telesales situation. Use storytelling to make the learner notice a problem, understand its causes, and see an actionable correction. Keep both substantial roleplay and substantial expert explanation.

## Core format

Use this skill's expert-led format when selected. Do not inherit the generic `storiestelling` skill's no-presenter default. Do not replace this format with a short two-person drama or a list of teaching points.

Read [references/approved-ensure-script.txt](references/approved-ensure-script.txt) before drafting the first full script in a session. It is the user-approved example of structure, depth, tone, and presentation. Its prices and offers are historical illustrative inputs, not a current product catalog. Learn the format without copying its plot or sales outcome into unrelated lessons.

## 1. Establish scope and preserve prior decisions

Extract the lesson, audience, source facts, product, call stage, cast, language, and requested length from the supplied material. Default to Vietnamese, milk-sales telesales, and an expert-led lesson of approximately 4–6 minutes when unspecified. Estimate again from the actual spoken text and acting pauses; never promise an exact duration without a timed read.

Use existing approved content as the baseline. Ask only when an unresolved fact prevents useful work. Mark missing commercial facts explicitly rather than inventing them.

- When asked to add an opening, write only that opening and its insertion point. Preserve approved roleplay and analysis verbatim.
- When asked to change presentation or identify speakers, relabel existing content without silently rewriting it.
- When asked for the full script or a file after several edits, assemble the complete approved script plus accepted changes. Do not export only the most recent fragment unless explicitly requested.
- Retain the approved customer, quantity, offer, setting, and scope. Do not infer a cast replacement or purchase purpose solely from a layout reference image.

## 2. Write a complete lesson, sections 0–7

### 0. Welcome and lesson introduction

Write an actual spoken greeting, e.g. “Chào mừng các bạn đến với chuỗi video thực hành kỹ năng telesales Ensure.” Name the lesson and what learners will practice, then bridge into the hook. Adapt the series/product name to the brief. A title card is not a spoken welcome. Keep this opening useful and brief, without giving away the entire analysis.

### 1. Hook through a familiar misconception

Let the expert describe a recognizable expectation and a frustrating contradiction: the customer needs milk and asks the price, but the call still ends in refusal. Raise a question the scene will answer. A teaser quote is optional; do not replay the same dialogue repeatedly just to lengthen the video.

### 2. State the practice situation

Explicitly explain who is buying, for whom, why now, what has already been discussed, what remains undecided, and what the consultant needs to do next. Do not jump into disconnected dialogue. Provide only context relevant to the skill; avoid invented family subplots or elaborate props designed solely to force emotion.

### 3. Play the complete unsuccessful attempt

Write enough turns for the audience to see the causal progression: a reasonable customer signal → a plausible consultant mistake → hesitation → a worsening response → a credible negative consequence.

Show the customer becoming less engaged through everyday language and reactions. A firm “Thôi, chị không mua đâu” is appropriate when supported by the preceding exchange. Do not soften every setback into a friendly question. Conversely, do not make the customer abusive or the consultant absurdly incompetent.

Let the scene play without expert interruptions. End with a brief reaction or pause before analysis. Never summarize the roleplay as “nhân viên chốt sai, khách từ chối”; write every spoken turn.

### 4. Explain the causes in depth

For each main mistake, have the expert:
1. Point to the exact line or observable action.
2. Explain what the customer knew and had not agreed to at that moment.
3. Explain how the consultant's response could cause uncertainty, pressure, or disengagement.
4. State the specific replacement behavior and why it addresses the same issue.

Write speakable paragraphs with transitions, not only labels such as “thiếu giá” or “hỏi yes/no.” Cover one or two connected errors thoroughly. Distinguish plausible interpretation of a fictional scene from universal claims about customers.

Teach the applicable formula using the scene's facts. For closing, review total payable, applicable discount/voucher, actual gifts, and shipping. Do not invent benefits just to fill all categories. Explain that assumptive closing proposes a concrete order for confirmation; it does not authorize an order without consent. Do not claim every yes/no question is wrong.

### 5. Replay correctly at the same decision point

Use a short expert bridge and an explicit replay transition. Preserve customer, need, objection, product, quantity proposal, price, and offer. Change the teachable behavior, not the conditions to manufacture success.

Write the entire corrected exchange. Let the customer still hesitate or ask relevant questions. Have the consultant answer, pause, propose clearly, and wait for confirmation. A fictional purchase can be an earned illustrative ending; never imply the method guarantees sales or fabricate a real recorded outcome.

### 6. Compare what changed

Have the expert compare the two attempts using specific lines, timing, information order, listening, and customer responses. Explain the mechanism of improvement, not just “lần này tốt hơn.” Keep the intended behavior distinct from the sales outcome.

### 7. Give the learner a concrete practice turn

Provide the customer cue, necessary facts, and a clear spoken instruction for the learner to respond. Include a pause-video cue or short practice hold and one memorable takeaway. Do not introduce a new unsupported offer or an unrelated test.

## 3. Make telesales speech and staging credible

- Keep consultant turns short, polite, and responsive, usually one to three sentences. Allow longer connected explanations for the expert; do not compress the whole lesson into short slogans.
- Use consistent forms of address, defaulting to “em – chị/anh” when supported by the cast. Keep objections colloquial. Avoid having customers diagnose the training rubric for the expert.
- Avoid arbitrary twists, a new discount that suddenly saves the sale, sentimental props, or a customer who becomes convinced merely because the consultant recites a formula.
- Use a fixed split-screen during roleplay unless requested otherwise: consultant at a workstation wearing a headset; customer elsewhere holding a phone. Preserve supplied screen sides and identities. They cannot see each other's physical actions; no dialogue reacting to unseen gestures or objects.
- Keep the phone/headset in use across clips; do not repeat the pickup action. Show listening, hesitation, interrupted typing, and changes of tone without exaggerated acting.
- Allow the expert to occupy a separate presenter shot before and after roleplay. A fixed roleplay layout does not prohibit expert-led segments.

## 4. Label all production layers

Provide full text, not an outline. For each scene or section, separate:

- `NGƯỜI NÓI: CHUYÊN GIA — TRỰC DIỆN`: presenter visible and speaking.
- `LỜI DẪN: CHUYÊN GIA — NGOÀI HÌNH`: exact narration over footage/graphics. Use only when intended; do not confuse narration with a stage direction.
- `NGƯỜI NÓI: [TÊN NHÂN VẬT]`: exact roleplay dialogue.
- `HÌNH ẢNH / HÀNH ĐỘNG`: non-spoken performance, camera, and edit notes.
- `CHỮ TRÊN MÀN HÌNH`: exact overlay text, or explicitly no extra overlay where useful.

Use labeled sections for readable scripts and TXT exports. Use a table if requested, with separate columns for speaker/mode, exact speech, visual direction, and screen text. Keep subtitles separate from teaching overlays. Add text and numeric graphics in post-production; do not ask generated characters to pronounce stage directions. Avoid simultaneous expert narration and character speech.

## 5. Check before delivering

Verify:
- Actual welcome and verbal introduction exist before the hook.
- The learner understands the situation before roleplay begins.
- Both roleplays contain full, natural exchanges with a credible setback and observable correction.
- Expert analysis cites the scene and explains why/how, in adequate depth.
- Wrong/right attempts preserve the same relevant commercial conditions.
- Totals, discounts, reward points, physical gifts, shipping, and consent are accurate to the supplied material. Do not stack conflicting promotions or invent nutritional/medical claims.
- Every spoken block has a named speaker and clear on-screen/off-screen mode; visuals and screen text cannot be mistaken for speech.
- Approved dialogue remains unchanged when the task only adds an introduction or reformats.
- Full exports include welcome plus all seven sections, not a recent fragment. Save TXT as UTF-8 and check section presence and final takeaway before delivery.

Use the user's current file-saving workflow for deliverables. For image approval, scene generation, or Google Flow handoff, continue with `video-spec` when requested; a script request alone does not authorize video generation. Do not report script completion as video completion.

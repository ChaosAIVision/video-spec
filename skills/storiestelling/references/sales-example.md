# Example: a closing conversation that stalls

Use this reference for sales-training stories. Adapt the dramatic mechanism, not the exact lines or a fixed sales outcome.

## Source discipline

Treat historical call prices as evidence about those calls, not current offers. Preserve call IDs privately for traceability when provided; do not display customer identifiers in a public video. Distinguish a cash discount, a voucher, reward points, and a physical gift. Do not stack offers from different examples. Check quantity × unit price, deductions, payable total, and shipping. State whether shipping is included or added.

If the brief contains conflicting promotion policies, identify the conflict and use placeholders until resolved. Do not quote an unsupported retail value for a gift. Do not turn nutrition claims into medical promises.

Assumptive closing means proposing a concrete order for confirmation, not registering an order without agreement. A yes/no question is not inherently wrong: final consent may be yes/no. The lesson is to avoid a vague close after consultation and help the customer evaluate a clear proposal.

## Dramatic premise

A new consultant thinks the call is nearly finished. The customer hesitates because the proposed order is unclear. The consultant notices the uncertainty, clarifies the concern, and presents one understandable option. The payoff is renewed engagement or confirmation, not a guaranteed purchase.

## Illustrative beat sketch

This is invented training dialogue, not a quotation from a real call. Bracketed commercial fields must be verified before recording.

| Beat | Action | Dialogue |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | The customer glances at the clock; the consultant stops smiling. | Customer: “Thôi, để chị tính lại đã em.” |
| Setup | A brief, clearly labeled rewind shows the preceding close. | Consultant: “Chị có muốn đặt luôn hai lon không ạ?” |
| Setback | Return to the present; the consultant almost repeats the offer, then pauses. | Consultant: “Dạ, chị đang cần cân nhắc thêm phần nào ạ?” |
| Turn | The customer reveals the missing information. | Customer: “Nãy giờ chị vẫn chưa rõ hai lon hết bao nhiêu, có tính thêm tiền giao không.” |
| Changed action | The consultant checks the verified offer and speaks more slowly. | Consultant: “Dạ, hai lon tổng [TỔNG TIỀN], [ƯU ĐÃI ĐƯỢC XÁC NHẬN]. Phí giao hàng [PHÍ SHIP]; tổng chị thanh toán là [TỔNG CUỐI]. Em lên đơn hai lon này cho chị nhé?” |
| Payoff | The customer puts down the pen and re-engages. | Customer: “Vậy chị hiểu rồi. Khoảng mấy ngày thì nhận được em?” |

The change is visible: the consultant stops repeating a vague purchase question and makes the proposal clear. The customer has not yet confirmed an order. Continue to verified delivery information and consent only if the story needs it.

## Adapt the second lesson without losing the story

For “closing without price or benefits,” begin with “Em đăng ký cho chị hai lon nha,” followed by the customer's natural concern about the total. Reveal the missing information through that interaction. Do not insert a presenter listing four mandatory offer components, and do not invent a gift or voucher just to fill a formula.

If the user explicitly wants a wrong/right replay, signal the rewind, retain the same facts and customer concern, and change only the teachable behavior. If a continuous story is requested, remove the rewind and let the hesitation lead directly into clarification.
